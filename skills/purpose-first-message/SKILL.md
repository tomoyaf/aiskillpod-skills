# Purpose-first message

Simon Sinek's Start with Why (2009) grew out of an idea he called the Golden Circle: three rings, with WHY at the centre, HOW around it and WHAT on the outside. WHAT is what an organization sells. HOW is the way it does things differently. WHY is its purpose or belief: why it exists beyond making money. Sinek argues that most organizations communicate from the outside in (what, then how, then sometimes why), while the ones that inspire loyalty communicate from the inside out, and that people buy into the belief as well as the product. This skill finds the why, how and what in what a company already says and does, then writes a message that starts from purpose and stays true.

## When to use

- A pitch, about page or homepage lists features and sounds like every competitor.
- Before a hiring push, a rebrand or a launch, to say why the company exists.
- When the team can't agree in one sentence why the company does what it does.
- Not for a line-by-line review of landing page copy: use landing-copy-review. Not for competitive positioning: use positioning-statement.

## What you need

- The current message (pitch, page, post) and where it will be used.
- The founding story: why the founders started, in their words.
- Decisions the company has made that cost it something (customers turned down, features refused, prices kept).
- What customers say they value, ideally quoted.
- Never invent a founding story or a belief the company doesn't act on. A why with no evidence stays a draft for the founders.

## Steps

1. **Sort the current message.** Label each sentence as what, how or why. Note which comes first.
2. **Find the why in evidence.** From the founding story and the costly decisions, write 2 or 3 candidate beliefs. Each must be backed by something the company did, not only said. Sinek's test is whether people inside can see it in decisions.
3. **Choose one why.** Pick the candidate best supported by evidence and most relevant to the audience. Write it as one sentence about what the company believes or the contribution it wants to make, without naming the product.
4. **Write the how.** List 2 to 4 ways the company acts on the belief, as behaviours: what it always does, or never does.
5. **Write the what.** The products or services, in one or two plain sentences, as proof of the why.
6. **Draft the message inside out.** Open with the why, follow with the how, end with the what and the ask. Make a short version (2 to 3 sentences) and a longer one for the page or pitch.
7. **Run the consistency check.** List any claim in the message the company doesn't act on, and any recent decision that contradicts the why. Fix the message or flag the decision for the founders.

## Output format

```markdown
# Purpose-first message: [company] for [use]

## Current message, sorted
| Sentence | What / how / why |
|---|---|
**Order today:** [e.g. what → how]

## Why candidates
| Belief | Evidence (a decision or action) |
|---|---|
**Chosen why:** [one sentence, no product]

## How (behaviours)
- We always / never [behaviour]

## What
[plain description]

## Message
**Short:** [2 to 3 sentences]
**Long:** [paragraph]

## Consistency check
- [claim without evidence, or a contradicting decision]
```

## Example

**Input:** "About page for our coffee roastery in Medellín: 'We roast specialty coffee in small batches. We ship within 48 hours. Subscriptions from 45,000 COP.' Founding story: my father grew coffee and was paid less than it cost him to grow it; I started the roastery to pay farmers properly. Decisions: we pay our 14 partner farms a published price above the market price [check: how much], we turned down a supermarket deal that required cutting that price, and every bag names the farm. Customers write that they like knowing who grew it."

**Output:**

```markdown
# Purpose-first message: coffee roastery, about page

## Current message, sorted
| Sentence | What / how / why |
|---|---|
| We roast specialty coffee in small batches. | what |
| We ship within 48 hours. | how |
| Subscriptions from 45,000 COP. | what |
**Order today:** what → how; no why.

## Why candidates
| Belief | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The people who grow coffee should earn a living from it | the published price to 14 farms; the supermarket deal turned down |
| Drinkers should know who grew their coffee | every bag names the farm; customers say they value it |
**Chosen why:** We believe the people who grow coffee should earn a good living from it.

## How (behaviours)
- We always publish what we pay our farms.
- We never accept a deal that lowers that price.
- We name the farm on every bag.

## What
Specialty coffee from 14 partner farms, roasted in small batches in Medellín and shipped within 48 hours, by the bag or by subscription.

## Message
**Short:** My father grew coffee and was paid less than it cost to grow. We believe the people who grow coffee should earn a good living from it. So we publish what we pay our 14 farms, name the farm on every bag, and roast in small batches in Medellín.
**Long:** [the short version, then the farms, the published price, the supermarket deal we turned down, then the subscription and the 48-hour shipping]

## Consistency check
- "A published price above the market price": confirm the figure and where it's published before saying it [check].
```

## Checks before you send

- [ ] Every why candidate cites a decision or action from the input.
- [ ] The chosen why doesn't name the product.
- [ ] The message opens with the why and ends with the what and the ask.
- [ ] The consistency check lists claims without evidence and decisions that contradict the why.
- [ ] No founding story, number or customer quote is invented.

## Common mistakes

- A why that is a goal ("to be the leading roastery"). Purpose is a belief or contribution, not a ranking.
- A why that nobody acts on. Without evidence, it reads as marketing.
- Burying the what. Readers still need to know what you sell and how to buy.
- Treating the Golden Circle as science. Sinek ties it to how the brain works, which scientists dispute; use it as a writing order, not a proof.
